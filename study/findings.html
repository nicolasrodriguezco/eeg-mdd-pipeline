Findings at a glance

Findings at a glance#

  1. No detectable diagnostic signal on unseen participants. The pre-specified primary model reached AUC 0.53 [0.36, 0.70] (\(p=0.456\)). No family of models (handcrafted features, Riemannian geometry or deep networks) did better than chance after correction.

  2. Demographics beat EEG. Age, sex and education alone reached AUC 0.78 [0.63, 0.91]. Adding EEG did not improve on that baseline. This result reflects how the sample was recruited, not a diagnostic signal.

  3. Trial-wise splitting manufactures accuracy. The same tangent-space model gave a participant-level AUC of 1.00 with a trial-wise split and 0.50 with a participant-wise split.

  4. The mechanism is person recognition. Single trials identify which of the 45 participants produced them with 97.9% accuracy (chance 2.2%).

  5. Muscle activity is a checkable confound. Complexity and high-frequency features tracked an EMG index computed on the raw recordings (Spearman ρ up to 0.59).

  6. Only large effects were detectable. Even an ideal, pre-trained score with true AUC 0.70 would pass the permutation threshold only 61% of the time with 18 patients. With the same assumptions, 80% power needs about 60 participants for AUC 0.70, 100 for 0.65 and 300 for 0.60.

  7. One hypothesis worth testing. A random forest on raw ERP waveforms was the only borderline EEG result (\(p=0.049\) with 40 permutations; Holm-corrected \(p=0.39\)). It is not a finding, but it is the most concrete lead for an independent, pre-registered test.

Points 5–7 come from post-hoc, exploratory analyses (audit) and are labelled as such throughout.
