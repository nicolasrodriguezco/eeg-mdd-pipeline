Deviations from the plan#

Each deviation records when it was made and what had been seen at that point. D6 (the audit) was made after all pre-specified results were known, and it is labelled exploratory throughout.

Deviations from docs/preregistration.md#

Each deviation is listed with when it was made and what had been seen at that point. None was made after seeing any feature distribution or model result.

D1 — Bad-channel detector replaced by PyPREP (2026-11-04)#

  • Seen at that point: only the preprocessing log (channel counts per subject). No features and no model results existed yet.

  • Problem: the pre-specified rule flagged up to 48 of 129 channels. The flagged channels included ones adjacent to the recording reference (13° from Cz) with negative median correlation with their neighbours. Of 441 flags, 414 came from the neighbour-correlation criterion.

    • This is a known failure mode. On reference-dominated data with large artefacts, whole-recording correlation is not a valid quality measure.

    • Under that rule, three HC participants would have been excluded for having more than 20% bad channels, because of a detector artefact.

  • Change: bad channels are detected with PyPREP NoisyChannels, the PREP pipeline of Bigdely-Shamlo et al. (2015), with RANSAC off. It flags channels that are NaN/flat or bad by deviation, HF noise, correlation, SNR, dropout or PSD, all with default thresholds. E129/Cz, the recording reference, is exempt because it is flat by construction.

  • Unchanged: the exclusion threshold (more than 20% bad channels) and every other step.

D2 — Re-run of 14 participants (infrastructure, not a methodological change)#

  • Problem: in the first run, 14 participants failed because PyTorch was being installed while ICLabel imported it (PermissionError on shm.dll).

  • Change: all 53 participants were re-run after D1.

D3 — Extra Riemannian variant (added before modelling)#

  • Change: ERPCovariances → tangent space → LR (pyRiemann) is added as a secondary trial-level model. Its class prototypes are estimated inside each training fold.

  • Why: it is the standard Riemannian pipeline for ERP data and complements the plain-covariance models. It is reported as secondary and exploratory.

D4 — Sensitivity analysis with all 53 participants (added before any model was run)#

  • Seen at that point: the preprocessing log only.

  • What happened: the pre-specified criteria excluded 8 of 53 participants: 6 of 24 MDD and 2 of 29 HC. In 7 of the 8 cases the reason was fewer than 240 retained trials.

    • The exclusion rate differs by group, so the exclusion itself is a potential selection bias.

  • Change: the primary model is also re-run on all 53 participants (all features, the same nested protocol) as a secondary sensitivity analysis.

  • Unchanged: the primary analysis still uses the 45 participants who meet the criteria.

D5 — Riemannian models: precomputed per-trial covariances and fixed C for ERPCov (2026-11-04, before any Riemannian result was seen)#

  • Reason: computational cost. Timing one outer fold showed TS-LR at ~210 s/fold (80 % spent recomputing per-trial OAS covariances in each of the 26 grid fits); with 10 repeats + 200 permutations (1050 outer folds) the three Riemannian models were projected at >12 h.

  • Change 1 (no effect on results): TS-LR and MDM receive per-trial OAS covariances computed once. Each covariance depends only on its own trial (no learning across samples), so this is leakage-free; the tangent-space reference mean, scaling, LR and MDM centroids remain fitted inside each training fold. A unit test (test_precomputed_cov_equivalent) verifies identical predictions.

  • Change 2: ERPCov-TS-LR uses fixed C = 0.1 (no inner grid), because its ERP prototypes are label-dependent and must be recomputed in-fold. C is fixed a priori, so this cannot inflate performance; at most it is slightly sub-optimal.

  • Change 3: per-worker RAM budget for trial-level models lowered from 1.2 to 0.75 GB (measured 0.65 GB), allowing more parallel workers.

D6 — Post-hoc audit analyses (2026-11-05, AFTER all pre-specified results were known)#

  • Seen at that point: every pre-specified result (primary AUC 0.53, demographics 0.78, all secondary models, confounds, interpretation). This deviation is therefore post hoc and everything it adds is exploratory.

  • Reason: an internal technical audit (reports/auditoria_paper.md) found that some questions a reviewer would ask were not answered by the pre-specified plan:

    • incremental value of EEG over demographics;

    • education as an EEG confound;

    • minimum detectable effect;

    • residual muscle activity as a confound;

    • quality and exclusions on all 53 participants;

    • theory-driven feature contrasts and aperiodic spectra;

    • task behaviour;

    • subject-level Riemannian representation;

    • subject identifiability;

    • learning curve;

    • permutation test of the raw-amplitude RF control.

  • Unchanged: the primary model, primary outcome, exclusion criteria, folds and every pre-specified number. Nothing pre-specified is replaced or re-ranked.

  • Not possible: the EGI simple-binary .raw files contain event onsets only (*fix, *cue, *isi, *dot, *wrp, resp, TRSP). The TRSP keys (congruency, side of the emotional face, accuracy) are not stored. A dot-probe attentional-bias index, congruent-vs-incongruent probe ERPs and an exact replication of Li et al. (2018) are therefore impossible with this release. Reaction time (*dot → resp) is available.

  • Multiplicity: the new exploratory classification models are Holm-corrected among themselves and reported separately from the pre-specified ones.

  • Raw-amplitude RF permutation budget: one full subject-wise run takes 159 s (measured), so 100 permutations would take about 4.4 h. 40 permutations were run instead, giving a minimum attainable p of 1/41 ≈ 0.024. This was decided from the timing alone, before any permuted AUC was seen.

  • Sample-size extrapolation (scripts/16_sample_size.py, added after the audit results were known): this is an analytic, optimistic lower bound. It uses an ideal binormal score with the class ratio 18/45, a one-sided α = 0.05 and the exact Mann–Whitney null. It makes no claim about the data.

  • Raw-RF Holm family: the RF permutation p is Holm-corrected together with the 7 exploratory models of the audit table, a family of 8.
